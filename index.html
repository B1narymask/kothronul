Gender is defined by the last vowel of the word.

Gender Vowel Example
Masculine a, o kotios (dog)
Feminine i, e cétir (cat)
Neuter u xesseud (house)

You might've noticed that none of the examples actually end in the vowel of their respective gender.

That's because they're not supposed to.

When I said "defined by the last vowel of the word", I meant the last vowel, not the last letter.

Lesson 3: Classes and Articles

Koþronul has 3 classes.

"but what are classes?"

Noun classes are basically just categories each noun is put into.

You can't just have a noun without a class.

In Koþronul, classes matter for articles and plurals.

The 3 classes respectively are: animate, inanimate & abstract


Koþronul hass a 3x3 article system: each gender and class combination has its own article.

for example, there's an article for masculine inanimate, feminine inanimate, neuter abstract, etc.

Here's the full table:

Gender / class Animate Inanimate Abstract
Masculine nox ca xlio
Feminine be yev ic
Neuter uhs uud stu

"but how can i tell which class a word is?"

Oh, that!

You kinda... Can't...

There really no way to consistently tell what class a word would be other than just guessing

romanization (or 'spelling' for normies):

'c' /t͡ʃ/ (English 'ch')

'þ' (or 'th') /θ/ (English 'th')

'x' /ʃ/ (English 'sh')

'y' /j/ (literally just y)

everything else is pronounced just about how you'd expect... except the vowels, they're pronounced almost completely different than English vowels
